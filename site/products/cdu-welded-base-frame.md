<h2 id="supply">Order the base underneath your CDU assembly</h2>

Order a custom welded CDU base frame for AI server and data-center liquid-cooling equipment, built to your approved assembly drawings. REALJET supplies the fabricated base with specified machined mounting interfaces; your CDU design defines the pump, heat exchanger and cabinet arrangement.

| Delivery condition | What to specify |
| --- | --- |
| Welded base frame | Plate and section drawings, braces, feet, weld requirements and dimensional limits |
| Base with machined mounting interfaces | Seating pads, locating features, holes, threads and the datum scheme |
| Coated base frame | Finish specification, colour, masked areas and final interface checks |

Include brackets, anchor plates, levelling provisions or drip-tray supports in the assembly drawing if you want them supplied. Pumps, heat exchangers, valves, pipework, controls and complete CDU assembly are outside the base-frame order unless separately agreed. A drip-tray support is a structural feature; it does not make the base a leak-tested vessel.

<h2 id="applications">Base frames for floor-standing CDUs and pump modules</h2>

### Floor-standing CDUs for AI server liquid cooling

If you build floor-standing coolant distribution units for liquid-cooled AI servers, the base sits beneath your equipment assembly. It provides the drawing-defined mounting structure for pump supports, heat exchanger supports and cabinet connections, together with the floor-fixing interface. REALJET supplies the fabricated metal base to your design; the CDU equipment manufacturer specifies the coolant circuit and cooling performance.

The equipment layout determines the frame. Pump-foot spacing sets the mounting-pad positions, the heat exchanger support arrangement sets the crossmember locations, and the cabinet drawing sets its attachment holes. Access for removing a pump or reaching a fastener also affects where braces and supports can sit.

### Separate pump-module bases within your CDU design

Where your CDU design uses a separately mounted pump module, its base can be reviewed as an individual weldment. Send the pump-foot pattern, any isolator details and the module-to-CDU connections so those interfaces can be included in the fabrication and machining scope.

Mark the installation and removal path in the assembly view. The review checks tool access around mounting bolts and clearance between the pump module, braces and adjacent equipment. Supply covers the base and agreed metal attachments; pump fitting and fluid-system work require a separate scope.

### How your installation requirements shape the part

| What your equipment needs | What changes on the base | Manufacturing and acceptance focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pump and heat exchanger supports at defined levels | Pad heights, support locations and crossmember layout | Locate the supports during welding; machine specified pads afterward and check their datum-based relationship |
| Cabinet and floor connections that fit the assembly | Cabinet fixing pattern, anchor feet and access around fasteners | Check hole positions, foot heights and mounting access against the approved assembly drawing |
| Room to service or replace a pump module | Brace locations, open access and clearance around mounting points | Review the removal path before fabrication; check fit with agreed mating parts or a fixture where required |

Send the assembly layout with the part drawings so these requirements can be reviewed together. The quoted base follows your equipment design rather than a standard footprint shared by every CDU.

<h2 id="interfaces">Keep pump, heat exchanger and cabinet interfaces on one drawing</h2>

For a floor-standing CDU, the frame must fit both the equipment above it and the installation beneath it. Show the mounting datums in the assembly view so the fabricated base can be checked in the same condition in which you will install it.

| Interface | Information needed for drawing review |
| --- | --- |
| Pump and any isolators | Foot pattern, seating levels, fastener access and clearance for maintenance |
| Heat exchanger supports | Mounting arrangement, support heights and permitted relative position |
| Cabinet attachment | Fixing pattern, panel clearances and access for assembly tools |
| Floor fixing and levelling | Anchor pattern, foot arrangement and specified measurement support points |

Your equipment engineer defines operating loads, transport loads and any lifting requirements. Send those requirements with the drawings; lifting capacity and vibration performance need a separately agreed verification plan.

<h2 id="process">Fabricate first, then finish the interfaces that control assembly</h2>

### Prepare the frame members and mounting plates

Laser or plasma cutting prepares plate profiles and connection features. Bending forms specified feet or brackets where the design calls for them. Identify purchased sections and material grades in the bill of materials. Part size, cut-edge requirements and machining allowance determine the selected route.

### Locate the supports before welding

The fixture locates the floor supports, crossmembers and equipment mounting pads from the drawing datums. Tack-up checks cover diagonals and support heights before the agreed welding sequence. Weld access, material and joint requirements determine the welding procedure; robotic welding is considered where access and repeat quantities suit the setup.

### Check the weldment before final machining

Check distortion after welding and before machining allowances are consumed. Review dimensional stability and whether the drawing requires stress relief. Any required stress-relief treatment and its provider must be confirmed in the quotation before manufacture.

### Machine specified pads, holes and threads

Mounting faces and holes can be finished after welding using gantry milling, boring or machining centres. The frame envelope, tool access and inspection requirements determine the machine and setup. The drawing identifies which surfaces need machining and which remain as fabricated.

### Finish and protect the mounting interfaces

Blasting, painting and powder coating are available for review against your finish specification. Agree the coating system for your installation and any required test results, with masking at seating faces, threads and electrical bonding points where specified. Recheck interfaces affected by coating or handling before packing.

<h2 id="inspection">Agree the first-article checks before the frame is made</h2>

| Feature to accept | Check to agree | First-article record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frame geometry | Overall dimensions, diagonals and foot heights on defined support points | Drawing revision, support condition and measured results |
| Mounting pads | Coplanarity or relative height against the specified datum scheme | Pad measurements and measurement method |
| Fixing and locating features | Hole sizes, datum-based positions, threads and fastener access | Dimensional results and thread checks |
| Welded joints | Visual checks against the specified weld acceptance criteria; additional NDT when required | Weld inspection results and any separately agreed NDT report |
| Finished surfaces | Specified coating thickness, masking, coverage and any required adhesion test | Finish inspection results under the agreed method |
| Assembly fit | Customer-supplied gauge, mating components or an agreed interface fixture | Separate fit-check record and outstanding deviations |

Datum-based inspection can use an articulated measuring arm where its working range and measurement uncertainty suit the specified features. Agree the measurement method and required calibration records before the order. A fit check complements dimensional inspection and does not replace it.

Request a first article or pilot quantity before repeat supply. Agree the inspection points and acceptance limits during drawing review. Approved changes become part of the controlled drawing revision for the next batch.

## Pack for the next assembly stage

Protect machined pads and threads from straps, moisture and contact with adjacent frames. Agree packing support locations so transport restraints do not distort the weldment. Supply your quantity, delivery city and required shipping terms for a quotation that includes the requested packing and freight scope.

## Questions about your CDU base frame

### Can you work from our CDU assembly drawings?

Yes. Send dimensioned part drawings and the assembly view, with material, weld details, datums and acceptance limits. STEP files help review access and assembly; the approved dimensioned drawings define what is inspected.

### Do we need every mounting pad machined?

Mark the pads that control equipment alignment and give their required relationship. The drawing review separates those interfaces from surfaces that can remain as fabricated, so machining is quoted where your assembly needs it.

### Can the base ship with the pump and heat exchanger fitted?

This page covers the welded base and agreed metal attachments. If you need mechanical fitting, provide the mating components and assembly instructions for a separate scope review. Hydraulic assembly, pressure testing and CDU commissioning require their own agreement.

### Can you guarantee a load rating or vibration limit?

Those values must come from your design and acceptance requirements. Provide the load cases, restraint conditions and test method for review. A base-frame order does not by itself certify the CDU's structural or vibration performance.
