Laser-cut accuracy alone does not define the tolerance of a finished bent or welded chassis.

<h2 id="sheet-metal-fabrication">What is sheet metal fabrication?</h2>

Sheet metal fabrication turns flat metal stock into functional parts through cutting, bending, joining and finishing. For a chassis, the flat pattern defines the openings and mounting features; bending creates the walls and flanges; joining and hardware installation prepare the part for equipment assembly.

In REALJET projects, this process supports control-system housings, machinery panels, equipment brackets and chassis subassemblies. Each part is evaluated against its installation space, operating environment and assembly requirements.

<h2 id="fabrication-technology">Sheet metal fabrication technology</h2>

1. **Drawing and manufacturability review.** Confirm the drawing revision, flat-pattern features, bend radii, material direction where relevant, hardware access and coating allowances.
2. **Cutting and edge preparation.** Laser-cut the specified profiles, slots and holes. Remove burrs and address exposed edges before forming or assembly.
3. **CNC bending and forming.** Establish the bend sequence and tooling around flange geometry and access. Check springback, bend dimensions and the relationship between mounting features.
4. **Joining and hardware installation.** Use specified welding, riveting or fastening methods. Select TIG, MIG or spot welding where appropriate to the material and joint design, and check installed inserts and studs.
5. **Surface finishing.** Prepare the surface and apply the agreed finish. Mask threads, fit surfaces and electrical contact areas where the drawing requires them.
6. **Assembly and inspection.** Check critical features and mating interfaces, then package the chassis to protect its geometry and finish during shipment.

<h2 id="materials-finishes">Materials and surface finishing</h2>

| Material | Selection considerations | Finishing options to review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Carbon / cold-rolled steel | Stiffness, forming requirements and corrosion protection | Powder coating, painting or zinc plating |
| Stainless steel | Grade, corrosion exposure, weld condition and appearance | Brushing, polishing or specified passivation |
| Aluminum | Weight, bendability, alloy and temper; electrical contact requirements | Anodizing, powder coating or painting |
| Galvanized steel | Coating condition at cut edges and joints; welding compatibility | As-specified sheet coating or additional protective finish |

Copper or brass parts can also be reviewed where electrical or thermal performance is required. The material and finish combination must suit the component: not every process is suitable for every grade. Specify color, texture, coating thickness and masking areas when appearance or assembly clearances are critical.

<h2 id="quality">Quality checks for chassis fit and assembly</h2>

Inspection should follow the features that matter to your installation. Overall dimensions, mounting-hole locations, bend angles, flatness where specified, hardware retention, edge condition and finish are typical review points. Where a mating part or gauge is available, agree the trial-fit method before production.

Material records, dimensional inspection reports, first-article documentation and additional measurements can be included when specified and confirmed in the supply scope. If your project requires a particular certification or material/finish compliance declaration, identify it at enquiry so the applicable evidence can be reviewed before order acceptance.

<h2 id="chassis-faq">Custom sheet metal chassis FAQ</h2>

### Which files should I send?

Send a dimensioned PDF and, where available, DXF/DWG profiles or a STEP model. Include the drawing revision, material, thickness, quantities and finishing requirements. A sample or sketch can support an initial discussion; the manufacturing definition must be agreed before production.

### Can the mounting holes and connector openings be changed?

Yes. The cut pattern and formed geometry can be customized to the approved design. Changes near bends, fasteners or edges need a manufacturability review before release.

### Can I start with a prototype?

A prototype or pilot batch can be reviewed before repeat production. Agree which dimensions, mating parts and functional checks the sample must satisfy, together with the approval process for the next batch.

### What determines price and lead time?

Material, part size, cut complexity, bend count, joining, hardware, finishing, quantity and inspection requirements all affect the quotation. Share the required delivery date so material availability and the production schedule can be checked.
