## Project-specific formwork for box girder production

Box girder moulds are used in precast factories and dedicated casting yards for highway, railway, urban viaduct and long-span bridge projects. Because web inclination, deck width, internal cell geometry, diaphragms, end details, prestressing ducts and lifting arrangements vary by project, the mould is engineered from the approved girder drawings and production method rather than supplied as a universal standard size.

A box girder mould system may include the bottom form, external side forms, internal forms or core sections, end forms, supporting structures, operating mechanisms and working platforms. The final division of the mould depends on the casting orientation, reinforcement installation sequence, concrete access, vibration method, demoulding clearance and component handling plan.

## Product performance and engineering features

- steel contact surfaces and supporting structures configured to the approved box-girder geometry;
- mould segmentation planned for practical assembly, alignment, concrete placement and demoulding;
- mechanical or hydraulic movement for selected side-form or internal-form functions where required;
- reference points, stops and locking devices for controlled setup between casting cycles;
- replaceable inserts or end-form arrangements for approved dimensional variants;
- coordinated interfaces for prestressing ducts, embedded items, vibration, curing and lifting operations;
- working access for preparation, inspection, cleaning and maintenance;
- workshop trial assembly and dimensional verification according to the agreed inspection scope.

Mould repeatability depends on correct setup, locking, inspection and maintenance. Final component tolerances and acceptance requirements must be defined by the project documents and confirmed in the mould design basis.

## Suitable production scenarios

The product is suitable for dedicated bridge casting yards, permanent precast plants and production lines manufacturing repeated full-length or project-defined box girders. Planning should consider the complete process, including reinforcement work, prestressing operations, concrete delivery, curing occupancy, crane or transfer coverage, demoulding sequence and storage or delivery routes.

To prepare a proposal, Realjet requires the girder drawings, component schedule, target cycle, concrete and prestressing information, required tolerances, mould operating preference, factory or yard layout and applicable project standards.
